Why I Started Software Engineering Newbies

Dec 12, 2026 • 5 min read • By Ankita Sharma

Before starting my first year of engineering, I was confused.

Like many students, I spent hours watching videos, searching online forums, and looking for concrete advice on how to actually succeed in engineering school.

Most of the guidance available focused purely on general study habits:

  • Make flashcards
  • Organize your Google Calendar
  • Time block your schedule
  • Stay organized with color coded notes

Do not get me wrong, those are great productivity techniques. But there was a much larger reality that nobody seemed to talk about openly.

"No gatekeeping. No ego. Just builders helping builders."

Software Engineering Is an Independent Journey

Success in software engineering does not happen solely inside the lecture hall.

Lectures, problem sets, and final exams are essential for your theoretical groundwork, but they represent only one part of the equation. What many students do not realize early enough is that landing internships and launching a career in tech requires self directed effort outside the classroom: learning modern tools, building original projects, practicing technical interviews, networking, and discovering which subfield genuinely excites you.

University coursework builds your foundational knowledge, but the independent craft you nurture outside the lecture hall is what truly helps you stand out.

The Real Problem Beginners Face

As an early student navigating university without a personal mentor in the tech industry, I frequently ran into confusing questions:

  • Which technical skills should I prioritize first?
  • How does the internship recruiting cycle actually operate?
  • What happens during technical interviews and online assessments?
  • What on earth is DSA, and why does everyone talk about it?
  • Which free learning platforms are genuinely worth my time?
  • How do I navigate between backend, frontend, cloud, and data paths?

There was an overwhelming amount of raw information across the web, but very little honest, structured guidance. It often felt as though everyone else had received an unwritten rulebook that I had missed.

Why I Built This Community

I started Software Engineering Newbies to create the exact space I wish had existed when I first stepped onto campus.

A dedicated hub where students and early career builders can find:

  • Actionable, unpretentious career roadmaps
  • Practical internship recruiting blueprints
  • Curated, zero cost learning resources
  • Hands on project ideas that build verified proof
  • Clear breakdowns of live technical and behavioural interviews
  • Honest overviews of diverse tech specializations

Above all, I wanted to build a culture free of gatekeeping and elitism. A welcoming space where beginners can ask fundamental questions without feeling self conscious or inadequate.

A Letter to My Younger Self

In many ways, every article, roadmap, and breakdown published here is simply a letter of guidance to my past self.

It is the practical roadmap I wish someone had placed in my hands before day one. If these lessons help even one student gain clarity, overcome imposter syndrome, and take confident action, then this community has fulfilled its purpose.

Thank you for being here, for reading, and for building alongside me.

Ankita Sharma
